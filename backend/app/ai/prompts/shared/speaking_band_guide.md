## Speaking assessment framework (paraphrased summary of the public IELTS Speaking criteria)
Four equally weighted criteria, each 0-9: Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, Pronunciation.

**Fluency & Coherence** - speaking at length without noticeable effort; whether hesitation is about content (natural) or about searching for language; logical development of answers; range of discourse markers. Band 7: speaks at length without noticeable effort or loss of coherence; some language-related hesitation; flexible use of connectives. Band 6: willing to speak at length but coherence may be lost through repetition, self-correction or hesitation. Band 5: maintains flow but relies on repetition, self-correction and slow speech; over-uses certain connectives.

**Lexical Resource** - range for different topics, precision, idiomatic language, ability to paraphrase. Band 7: flexible vocabulary for a variety of topics; some less common and idiomatic items; effective paraphrase. Band 6: wide enough vocabulary to discuss topics at length; meaning clear despite inappropriacies. Band 5: manages familiar topics but with limited flexibility.

**Grammatical Range & Accuracy** - range of complex structures and the frequency of error-free sentences. Band 7: a range of complex structures with some flexibility; frequent error-free sentences. Band 6: a mix of simple and complex structures with limited flexibility; errors are frequent in complex structures but rarely impede communication. Band 5: basic sentence forms with reasonable accuracy; limited complex structures.

**Pronunciation** - intelligibility, word and sentence stress, intonation, connected speech. It can only be judged from audio. LinguaSI usually receives transcripts, so pronunciation must NOT be inferred from text.

Test structure: Part 1 (4-5 minutes) familiar topics; Part 2 (1 minute preparation, 1-2 minutes long turn on a cue card); Part 3 (4-5 minutes) abstract discussion linked to Part 2.
